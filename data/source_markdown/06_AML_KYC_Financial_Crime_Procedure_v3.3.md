## 1. Risk-Based Approach

This section defines how North Pennine Community Credit Union (NPCU) applies risk-based approach in day-to-day operations. The control intent is to make decisions repeatable, evidenced and capable of independent review.

Operational teams must record enough evidence for a reviewer to reconstruct the decision without relying on the memory of the original colleague. Where a system rule and a documented policy appear to conflict, the current approved policy takes precedence and the discrepancy must be raised as a change or incident.

Managers are expected to monitor both individual exceptions and emerging patterns. A single exception may be reasonable; a recurring exception can indicate a control design problem, training issue, configuration defect or change in member behaviour.

### Key controls

- **1.1** Financial-crime controls must be proportionate to the member, product, transaction and delivery-channel risk.
- **1.2** A low-risk member still requires core identity verification.
- **1.3** Higher-risk indicators require enhanced review rather than arbitrary assumptions.

Control 1. Financial-crime controls must be proportionate to the member, product, transaction and delivery-channel risk. The case record must show the information relied upon, the outcome, the colleague or automated rule that made the decision, and any approval required. Evidence should be captured at the point of decision rather than reconstructed later.

Quality assurance should test a representative sample of cases against this control. Where an error could affect a member financially, regulatory obligations, data integrity or the accuracy of management reporting, the issue must be corrected promptly and considered for wider impact assessment.
