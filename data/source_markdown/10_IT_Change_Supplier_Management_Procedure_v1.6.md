## 1. Change Classification

This section defines how North Pennine Community Credit Union (NPCU) applies change classification in day-to-day operations. The control intent is to make decisions repeatable, evidenced and capable of independent review.

Operational teams must record enough evidence for a reviewer to reconstruct the decision without relying on the memory of the original colleague. Where a system rule and a documented policy appear to conflict, the current approved policy takes precedence and the discrepancy must be raised as a change or incident.

Managers are expected to monitor both individual exceptions and emerging patterns. A single exception may be reasonable; a recurring exception can indicate a control design problem, training issue, configuration defect or change in member behaviour.

### Key controls

- **1.1** Changes are classified as standard, normal or emergency according to risk, repeatability and urgency.
- **1.2** A change affecting lending rules, payment files or member data is never treated as low impact solely because the code change is small.
- **1.3** Dependencies and rollback requirements must be identified before deployment.

Control 1. Changes are classified as standard, normal or emergency according to risk, repeatability and urgency. The case record must show the information relied upon, the outcome, the colleague or automated rule that made the decision, and any approval required. Evidence should be captured at the point of decision rather than reconstructed later.
